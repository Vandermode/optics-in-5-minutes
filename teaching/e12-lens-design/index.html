E12 / WHY LEARN THIS?

Which lens is better for the image you need?

Two lenses can have the same focal length yet form different finite-aperture spots. Improving the centre may worsen the edge. A useful design process must state the required field, pupil and image plane before it changes the surface shapes.

What you gain: Turn an imaging requirement into a reproducible objective, optimize within physical constraints and explain the tradeoffs that remain after the score improves.

Unpack the difficult steps

What does a centroid-based RMS spot reward, and what does it ignore?

0:21 · Replay this part

x̄ₖ = Σⱼ wⱼ xₖⱼ / Σⱼ wⱼ
σₖ² = Σⱼ wⱼ |xₖⱼ − x̄ₖ|² / Σⱼ wⱼ
J = Σₖ Wₖ σₖ² / Σₖ Wₖ

The pupil weights wⱼ describe sampling within one field. The field weights Wₖ specify which object directions matter to the design. Subtracting each field’s centroid removes its image location from this blur metric, so distortion is not constrained. A mean of variances also differs from a worst-field objective.

All fields share one image plane. Giving each field its own best focus would remove a limitation that a camera with one flat sensor still has. The search may choose that common plane, but it may not assign a different one to every field.

How can a lens change shape without changing its focal length?

1:02 · Replay this part

q = (c₁ + c₂)/(c₁ − c₂), cᵢ = 1/Rᵢ
Φ = (n − 1)[c₁ − c₂ + (n − 1)d c₁c₂/n]

At fixed n, d and paraxial power Φ, there are still different ways to distribute the two surface curvatures. Solving the power constraint for each q produces a family of prescriptions. Their finite-angle aberrations and principal-plane positions differ, even though their effective focal length is the same.

What keeps the optimizer from finding a misleading shortcut?

3:07 · Replay this part

Keep the pupil fixed and reject or penalize failed traces. Check surface separation over the full mechanical clear aperture. Re-evaluate the selected prescription with denser and shifted pupil samples, and with object directions between the optimized fields. These checks distinguish an optical improvement from a sampling accident.

A low geometric RMS is not a modulation-transfer measurement or a tolerance analysis. E03 provides the diffraction connection, while E10 explains objective sensitivities in a different forward model. A ray optimizer and a Maxwell adjoint share a design workflow without being the same algorithm.

01 / BUILD AN INTUITION

What to notice first.

E11 uses ray tracing to predict where a chosen lens sends each ray. Inverse design turns that calculation into a decision: which permitted prescription makes a specified image metric better? A search algorithm cannot decide what better means until the objective and design constraints are written down.

This lesson keeps the model deliberately small. A singlet in air has index n = 1.5, centre thickness d = 4 mm and paraxial effective focal length f = 50 mm. We vary the two surface curvatures together and choose one image plane for the whole field. The entrance stop stays at z = −5 mm. Glass type, element count and aperture are not optimized.

c₁ = 1/R₁,   c₂ = 1/R₂,   q = (c₁ + c₂)/(c₁ − c₂)

The bending parameter q changes how the refracting power is shared between the front and rear surfaces. The curvatures are solved together so the thick-lens focal power remains fixed. The back focal distance can still move: a fixed effective focal length does not fix the principal planes.

For each object field angle we trace a weighted pupil bundle to the same image plane. The centroid gives the image location. The mean squared distance about that centroid is the geometric spot variance; its square root is the RMS spot radius. The multi-field objective averages the variance over 0°, 3° and 6° with equal field weights.

J = Σₖ Wₖ σₖ² / Σₖ Wₖ,   σₖ = RMS radius about field k’s centroid

By the end, you can

  1. Separate a lens prescription, a performance metric and the constraints that keep the task unchanged.
  2. Compare on-axis and multi-field optimization using one common image plane and the same entrance aperture.
  3. Audit an improved design with denser pupil sampling and explain the image-quality questions its merit function omits.

03 / CONNECT THE LOGIC

A prescription says what may change.

The ray tracer reads surface radii, axial positions, index and clear apertures. The design parameterization must turn every trial into a valid prescription. Here the focal-power constraint is built into that parameterization; positive edge thickness and complete tracing are checked for every trial.

(50 mm)⁻¹ = (n − 1)[c₁ − c₂ + (n − 1)d c₁c₂/n]

For a fixed prescription, moving the image plane changes the transverse intercepts of all rays. Refocusing is therefore part of this design calculation. Every evaluated field must use the same plane; independently refocusing each field would hide field curvature and evaluate a different instrument.

The objective decides which compromise the search prefers.

An on-axis objective gives no direct reward for an improved edge field. Including several fields changes the question the search answers. Averaging squared spot radii also differs from minimizing the worst field; changing these weights is a design decision, not a plotting option.

This RMS metric subtracts each field’s own centroid. It therefore measures geometric spread without penalizing the image’s displacement from an ideal height. Distortion, relative illumination, chromatic performance and modulation transfer require additional measurements or objectives.

A search must not improve the score by changing the task.

The entrance aperture stays fixed throughout a run. Rays that miss an allowed surface or fail to transmit are counted as failures and penalized, not silently removed before computing a smaller spot. The lens surfaces must retain physical clearance over the mechanical aperture.

The browser uses a bounded numerical search in this two-parameter family, with one common refocus. E10 instead demonstrates adjoint derivatives for a phase-pupil wave model. The shared idea is the forward-model–objective–update loop; more complex optical systems may need different derivatives and search methods.

Validate beyond the samples that chose the design.

Re-evaluate the final prescription with more pupil rays and intermediate field angles. Report the common image plane, field weights, ray count and failed-ray fraction. A lower sampled merit establishes improvement for that model and objective; it does not establish a global optimum, a manufacturable camera or a diffraction PSF.
