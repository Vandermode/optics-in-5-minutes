E13 / WHY LEARN THIS?

Which optical parameter should change to improve the measured image?

Trying every lens curvature or phase coefficient separately becomes expensive. A derivative connects a measured error to its causes in the optical model, so a design update can follow a calculated direction.

What you gain: Build and verify a differentiable optical calculation, understand its relation to adjoints, and identify what is needed before extending it to joint optics and reconstruction design.

01 / BUILD AN INTUITION

What to notice first.

E12 asks what makes a lens better; E10 shows how an adjoint obtains many sensitivities at once. Differentiable optics connects these ideas: write the optical prediction as a sequence of operations, and carry the loss sensitivity back to the physical parameters you can change.

θ = (a, b) → φ(x) → u(x) → v(q) → I(q) → L

Here x is normalized pupil position, q labels sampled focal-plane spatial frequencies, a and b are real phase coefficients in radians, u is the pupil field, v is the focal field, and I is the intensity. A fixed aperture and incident power keep the task unchanged. The target I★ is the focused pattern of the same aperture with a chosen tilt a★.

φ(x) = ax + bx²;   u = A exp(iφ);   v = F u;   I = |v|²

F is a unitary discrete Fourier transform. The pupil amplitude A is fixed and normalized so ΣA² = 1; consequently ΣI = 1. The camera measures squared field magnitude. It does not directly measure phase, but interference during propagation makes the intensity depend on phase.

By the end, you can

  1. Trace the dependency from real design parameters through a complex field to a real loss.
  2. Check a reverse-mode gradient with finite differences before using it to update a design.
  3. Distinguish differentiable optics, automatic differentiation, adjoint methods and end-to-end co-design.

03 / CONNECT THE LOGIC

Differentiate the actual measured quantity.

S = Σq I★(q)²;   L(θ) = Σq [I(q; θ) − I★(q)]² / (2S);   r = (I − I★)/S

S is the fixed squared norm of the target. Dividing by S makes the dimensionless loss relative to that target; S is held constant when differentiating the design. Its value compares the full sampled pattern, not just the peak. Real cameras also introduce pixel integration, noise and saturation; this core model omits them.

At a detector sample, a positive residual means that the prediction is too bright. Since dL = Σ r dI, the field sensitivity contains r ⊙ v. The conjugate transpose F† then combines the contribution of every detector sample at each pupil position.

dL = 2 Re[(r ⊙ v)† dv] = 2 Re[w† du]
dI = 2 Re(v* ⊙ dv);   w = F†(r ⊙ v);   gφ = 2 Im(u* ⊙ w)

The star denotes complex conjugation, ⊙ pointwise multiplication, and † the conjugate transpose. Move the field sensitivity back through F using F†, then use du = i u dφ. This gives the real phase sensitivity gφ. The backward field is a mathematical sensitivity, not light literally travelling backwards through the experiment.

∂L/∂a = Σx gφ(x)x;   ∂L/∂b = Σx gφ(x)x²

This final chain-rule step is the new connection to design. E10 allowed each phase pixel to change independently; here only two physical phase shapes may change. Sum the pixel sensitivities with the derivatives of those shapes. A constant phase offset would have zero intensity sensitivity, so it is not a useful design variable for this task.

Check the derivative before trusting an update.

gⱼ ≈ [L(θ + h eⱼ) − L(θ − h eⱼ)] / (2h);   θnew = θ − ηg

eⱼ selects one parameter and h is a small perturbation in radians. Too large a step adds truncation error; too small a step subtracts nearly equal floating-point numbers. Sweep several h values in the notebook. The optimizer separately chooses η by backtracking and accepts a step only after recomputing a lower loss. A correct gradient is local information, not a guarantee of a global optimum.

At the default initial design, the loss gradient initially asks for more defocus while reducing tilt. The two parameters act together on the pattern: descent reduces their combined loss, not necessarily each coefficient separately. Try the difficult starting point to see a nonzero plateau even though a zero-loss target is known.

Three related ideas have different roles.

Differentiable optics is an optical model with usable derivatives with respect to chosen design variables. Automatic differentiation applies local derivative rules to a computation graph. An adjoint method efficiently evaluates the sensitivity of a scalar objective, often through an implicit field solve. They can be combined: an optical solver may supply a custom adjoint derivative inside an automatic-differentiation system. This experiment writes reverse-mode derivatives explicitly; it does not call an autodiff library.

The same chain can pass through curved lenses.

In E11, an intersection satisfies S(r₀ + td; θ) = 0. If its derivative with respect to t is nonzero and the same intersection branch remains selected, implicit differentiation gives dt/dθ = −(∂S/∂θ)/(∂S/∂t), with the incoming ray held fixed for this local derivative. The complete chain must also differentiate the incoming ray and Snell refraction. Grazing incidence, clipping, total internal reflection and switching intersections can invalidate a smooth local derivative.

End-to-end design changes where the loss is measured.

y = Hθ(s);   ŝ = Rψ(y);   Ltask = ℓ(ŝ, s)

s is a scene, Hθ the optical and sensor model, y the measurement, and Rψ a reconstruction with digital parameters ψ. Joint design differentiates the final task loss through both Rψ and Hθ, updating optics and reconstruction together. A sharper raw PSF is then not automatically the best task result. The present browser experiment optimizes an optical intensity pattern only; it is not a trained reconstruction system.

Hold the scene distribution, illumination, aperture, exposure and sensor model fixed when comparing designs. Evaluate held-out scenes and physical tolerances. Hard phase rounding or changing a ray path is not made differentiable merely by placing it in a computation graph: use a justified smooth model during optimization and check the final discrete or fabricated design separately.
